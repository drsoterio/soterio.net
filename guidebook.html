Participatory Guidebook on Science Communication

Bridging Theory and Practice Through UX Research and Instructional Design

The First SciComm Instructional Material to Consider Students' Needs

Science communication is a critical skill for STEM students, yet many training programs fail to address their specific needs. This participatory guidebook bridges the gap between theory and practice by combining UX research, instructional design, and design thinking to create a resource tailored to students' unique challenges and aspirations. Students and practitioners actively shaped the guidebook from start to finish, ensuring an user-centered approach.

Understanding the Problem

Undergraduate STEM students face several challenges in science communication, as highlighted by the six strands of science communication learning goals:

  • Affective Goals: Students want to feel excitement and motivation about science communication but often lack structured training and mentorship to build confidence.
  • Content Goals: They seek to learn both science communication theory and foundational science, aiming to connect their knowledge to real-world applications.
  • Methods Goals: Many struggle with audience-specific messaging and want to expand beyond written media to explore storytelling through podcasts, videos, and social media.
  • Participation Goals: Students desire real-world opportunities to practice science communication, such as writing for public audiences, creating multimedia content, and engaging in dialogues with diverse communities.
  • Reflection Goals: They want to understand the societal role of science and reflect on accessibility challenges, such as how science communication can reach marginalized audiences.
  • Identity Goals: Despite their engagement, many students do not identify as science communicators due to a lack of confidence, training, and clear career pathways.

Designing the Guidebook

Using a design thinking approach, I developed the guidebook through the following steps:

  • Empathize: Conducted workshops and interviews with students, educators, and practitioners to understand their needs and challenges.
  • Define: Framed the challenge around the six strands of science communication learning goals: Affective, Content, Methods, Participation, Reflection, and Identity.
  • Ideate: Collaborated with students, instructors, journalists, industry professionals, and policymakers to brainstorm content, structure, and interactive elements.
  • Prototype: Created a modular guidebook with activities, templates, and multimedia examples.
  • Test: Iterated based on feedback to refine usability and effectiveness.

Key Features of the Guidebook

  • Modular Structure: The guidebook is structured around the classic "Who, What, When, Where, Why, and How" framework, providing a clear and intuitive organization for users to navigate science communication concepts and practices.
  • Interactive Elements: Activities, templates, media hyperlinks, and reflection prompts to encourage active learning.
  • Multimedia Integration: Examples of science communication in various formats (e.g., podcasts, videos, infographics) to inspire creativity.
  • Real-World Scenarios: Includes real-world examples from published science communication media (e.g., articles, podcasts, videos), and highlighted participants' comments on central topics

Outcome: A User-Centered Resource

The final guidebook aimed to empower students to:

  • Develop confidence and skills in science communication.
  • Bridge the gap between theory and practice.
  • Engage in real-world science communication activities.
  • See themselves as science communicators.

How the Guidebook Works

The guidebook is designed to bridge the gap between theory and practice, combining UX research and instructional design principles to create a resource that is both engaging and effective:

  • User-Centered Design: Built around the needs of STEM students, the guidebook prioritizes empathy and real-world applicability, ensuring it addresses the challenges users face in science communication.
  • Structured Learning: The content is organized to facilitate skill development, with clear pathways for users to build confidence and competence in science communication.
  • Iterative Development: Through a participatory design process, the guidebook was refined based on feedback from students, educators, and practitioners, ensuring it meets the needs of its audience.

Why This Matters

This guidebook is more than just a resource—it’s a tool for transformation:

  • Practical and Actionable: It provides step-by-step guidance, interactive activities, and real-world examples to help users apply what they learn.
  • Inclusive and Collaborative: By incorporating diverse perspectives and scenarios, it ensures relevance for a wide range of users, from beginners to advanced learners.
  • Innovative and Engaging: The use of multimedia, case studies, and participatory design makes the guidebook a dynamic and inspiring resource.
