About Me

I am a doctoral researcher in the Communication, Media, and Learning Technologies Design program at Teachers College, Columbia University. I study emancipatory STEM learning—how learners reclaim science and technology as tools for critical thinking and collective agency. Grounded in participatory methods and constructionist practices, my work investigates how sociotechnical systems—interwoven networks of people, communities, norms, and technologies (digital or otherwise), such as AI, social media, and hands-on science tools—can be reimagined to foster equity and critical participation. I don’t just respond to existing systems; I invent new ones.

I am the inventor of Lab-on-a-Book, a paper-based chemistry kit designed to democratize science learning through low-cost, high-engagement experimentation, and a member of the Paulo Freire Initiative at Columbia University. Previously, I’ve produced guidebooks, podcasts, and science kits; co-created the Global Conversation on Sustainability with IUPAC; and co-founded Brazil’s first American Chemical Society (ACS) Student Chapter, becoming the first international student to receive the ACS Leadership Award.
