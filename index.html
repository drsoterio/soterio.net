Building tools_
for science learning

learning scientist/chemist/science communicator

I'm a PhD student in Technology, Media, and Learning at Teachers College, Columbia University, advised by Paulo Blikstein in the Transformative Learning Technologies Lab. I study how the design, interfaces, and algorithmic architectures of physical toolkits, AI, and social networks shape how the public participates in scientific practices.

My work draws on constructionism, critical pedagogy, and sociotechnical perspectives on learning. I'm the inventor of Lab-on-a-Book and a program coordinator at the Paulo Freire Initiative.

Things I'm building_

Tools Competition 2026 winner
lab-on-a-book · inventor

A real chemistry lab, printed on paper.

Lab-on-a-Book is an ultra-low-cost, paper-based science platform that lets students do real experiments, from cyanotypes and chromatography to pH and microbiology, at home or in schools that have no lab. Lab-on-a-Bot, an AI curiosity companion, guides students with questions instead of handing them answers.

  • awardTools Competition winner (USD 50,000), Renaissance Philanthropy & The Learning Agency
  • researchConstructionism 2025; three ISLS 2026 papers, including an Outstanding Student Poster Award

What I work on_

Three threads, one question: how do the tools we build shape who gets to take part in science?

01

Low-cost tools & boundary objects

Designing paper-based, pocketable labs and studying how students use the same experiments at home and in the classroom.

  • Lab-on-a-Book (Constructionism 2025)
  • Students' journeys with boundary tools (ISLS 2026)
  • A pocketable, low-cost lab (ISLS 2026)
02

AI in science inquiry

How learners question, test, and make sense of AI chatbots during inquiry, and whether they treat the AI as a friend, an instructor, a browser, or a partner.

  • Interaction modes in student-AI conversation (NARST 2026)
  • Mental models of AI chatbots (ISLS 2026)
03

Platforms, publics & democracy

How social platforms and algorithms shape collective knowledge-building, from far-right YouTube comments to critical pedagogy under algorithmic constraints.

  • Co-constructing (twisted) knowledge (ISLS 2025)
  • Can critical pedagogy survive algorithmic constraints? (ISLS 2026)
